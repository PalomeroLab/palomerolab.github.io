# Teresa

Dr. Palomero is a Professor of Pathology and Cell Biology at the Institute for
Cancer Genetics at Columbia University Irving Medical Center (CUIMC).

She trained at the University of Oviedo (Spain) and completed postdoctoral
fellowships at Harvard Medical School before joining Columbia in 2005, where she
leads a research program dedicated to understanding, and ultimately treating,
T-cell malignancies.

## Bio

Over two decades, her lab has leveraged the power of next generation sequencing
approaches to identify novel oncogenes and tumor suppressors in T-ALL
([Van Vlierberghe et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.542)) and peripheral
T-cell lymphomas ([da Silva Almeida et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3442)),
as well as drivers of resistance to chemotherapy in relapsed leukemias
([Tzoneva, et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm.3078); [Oshima et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1608420113)).
These genetic drivers of T-ALL and PTCL include RHOA G17V, VAV1 fusions, and
PHF6 mutations, and they underpin some of the field's first mouse models of
angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma.

Her current research focuses on the elucidation of the oncogenic mechanisms
driven by these genetic alterations, the development of genetically manipulated
mouse models of T-cell lymphoma and the therapeutic targeting of key oncogenic
pathways responsible for T-cell transformation. Her work is funded by the NIH and
Blood Cancer United, and she serves as a Scientific Editor for _Blood Cancer
Discovery_. Dr. Palomero also serves as **Director of Graduate Studies for the
Cancer Biology track** of Columbia's VIPRE graduate program and Course Director
of Cancer Biology I, reflecting a long-standing commitment to mentoring
early-career scientists.
