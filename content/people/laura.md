## Bio

Dr. Laura Quevedo earned her B.S. in Biology from the University of Oviedo and
completed her Ph.D. in Molecular Biology and Biomedicine at the University of
Cantabria under the mentorship of Dr. Ignacio Varela. During her doctoral
research, she investigated the role of genetic and functional intratumoral
heterogeneity in pancreatic cancer using genetically engineered mouse models,
lineage tracing, and next-generation and single-cell sequencing approaches.

Dr. Quevedo is currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Palomero Laboratory at
Columbia University, where she studies the role of chromatin regulators in
hematopoietic stem cell biology and T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia
(T-ALL). Her research combines genetically engineered mouse models,
competitive transplantation assays, CRISPR-based functional genomics,
genome-wide genetic screens, and single-cell and bulk transcriptomic
approaches to investigate how PHIP regulates hematopoietic stem cell aging and
leukemogenesis, as well as its functional relationship with the chromatin
regulator PHF6.
